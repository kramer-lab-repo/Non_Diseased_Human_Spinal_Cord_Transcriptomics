# Characterizing the Molecular and Cellular Biology of the Non-diseased Adult Human Spinal Cord 

# Authors 
Xindi Wang, Paulina S. Scheuren, Ryan W. J. Loke, Amrit Singh, Catrina Loucks, Min Hyung Ryu, John L. K. Kramer 

# Overview 
This repo contains the RNA seq analysis pipeline for exploring non-diseased adult human spinal cord transcriptome with regards to sex, age and level. 

# Manuscript Abstract 
The adult human spinal cord is integrally involved in sensory, motor and autonomic function. Compared with the brain, molecular and cellular characteristics that define the non-diseased spinal cord are poorly understood. This study aims to address this knowledge gap by integrating three publicly available bulk RNA-sequencing datasets comprising 249 non-diseased spinal cord samples. To characterize molecular and cellular signatures, we performed differential gene expression analysis (DGE), gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and deconvolution analysis to examine the effect of sex, age and anatomical spinal cord level. DGE revealed a distinct and orderly expression of HOX genes by level, and a greater number of genes affected by age compared with sex. GSEA revealed an enrichment of T cell activation pathways among older individuals and synaptic transmission pathways among younger individuals. Deconvolution analyses revealed increased macrophage abundance with increasing age, and cell-type composition variation by level. Our findings highlight that non-diseased adult human spinal cord biology is shaped predominantly by aging and level, with sex contributing more subtle effects. These findings establish a reference framework for non-diseased adult human spinal cord biology and provide an important foundation for future studies investigating spinal cord disorders and neurodegenerative diseases.
